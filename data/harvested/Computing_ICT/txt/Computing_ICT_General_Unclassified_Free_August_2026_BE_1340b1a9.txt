Junior High School final-year students preparing for the Basic Education Certificate Examination (BECE) now have access to a newly released assessment resource. The

Set by experienced examiners, the 1-hour 45-minute assessment package is structured to mirror current regional testing standards
. It targets core competencies spanning geography, civic education, environmental management, and human rights, providing an essential benchmark for upcoming national examinations.
Breakdown of the Assessment Structure
The released Social Studies paper is divided into two primary components designed to test both factual recall and critical analytical skills.
Paper 2: Essay Section
The essay component requires candidates to answer three questions in total: one compulsory question from Section I and two selective questions chosen from Sections II and III respectively
.
Section I (Compulsory Map and Environmental Study):
Features a map-reading exercise centered on Ghana’s climatic regions (marked I, II, III, and IV) where students must identify regions and state their physical characteristics
. This section also addresses critical environmental topics, requiring candidates to explain two practical methods of controlling flooding
.
Section II (Geographical Landscapes & National Institutions):
Asks students to define physical, drainage, and relief features with examples, alongside outlining the core institutional functions of the Ghana Meteorological Service and the National Disaster Management Organization (NADMO)
. Alternatively, candidates can tackle questions on community leadership, positive attitudes for peacebuilding, and conflict management methods
.
Section III (Human Rights and Community Initiatives):
Focuses on human rights, including justifications for when a person’s rights may be lawfully restricted and ways to protect rights within a community
. It also examines the distinction between community decisions and community initiatives, alongside the benefits of local development projects
.
Paper 1: Objective Test
The multiple-choice section contains 40 questions (A to D) designed to test foundational knowledge across civic responsibilities, environmental hygiene, sanitation, moral education, and cultural understanding
. Notable scenarios evaluate students’ comprehension of social responsibility (such as clean-up exercises), human resource development, water pollution causes, environmental degradation from chemical fertilizers, and the socio-economic impacts of rural-urban drift
.
Why This Practice Paper Matters for Exam Preparation
With the BECE serving as a critical transition milestone for Ghanaian students, mock examinations function as an indispensable diagnostic tool. Educators note that practicing with examiner-set questions allows candidates to:
Identify Knowledge Gaps:
Detailed review of mock responses helps pinpoint misunderstood geographical concepts, such as climatic zones and relief features
.
Master Time Management:
The strict 1-hour 45-minute window for Paper 2 trains students to organize, structure, and articulate comprehensive essay points efficiently.
Familiarize with Marking Standards:
Engaging with structured questions aligned with official examining patterns builds psychological readiness and reduces exam-day anxiety.
