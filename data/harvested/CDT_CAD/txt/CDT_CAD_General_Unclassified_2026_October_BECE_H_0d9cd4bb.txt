Enrol Your Ward for 2026 October BECE Home Mock Now
by
Wisdom K.E Hammond
·
October 1, 2026
Parents of BECE candidates are being encouraged to enrol their wards for the
October 2026 BECE Home Mock
to help students begin or strengthen their examination preparation early and give families a structured way to monitor progress ahead of the Basic Education Certificate Examination (BECE).
The October 2026 Home Mock examination starts on Friday, 9th October, 2026. Get in touch with us on 0550360658 for a super discount for your first mock registration and package.
The BECE Home Mock is designed to give students an opportunity to practise examination questions under a structured mock examination arrangement while helping parents assess their wards’ performance and identify areas that require further attention.
For parents whose wards are already preparing for the BECE, the October mock provides another opportunity to test their readiness, while students who are starting their preparation early can use the exercise to become familiar with the demands of the examination.
Why parents should enrol their wards for the October BECE Home Mock
One of the main benefits of an early mock examination is that students do not have to wait until the final stages of BECE preparation before discovering the subjects or areas where they need improvement.
By participating in the BECE Home Mock, students can practise answering examination questions and use their performance to identify areas that need more study.
For parents, the mock can provide a more structured way to follow their wards’ preparation. Instead of relying only on classroom work or informal revision at home, parents can use mock performance as an additional reference point when discussing their wards’ academic progress.
The October exercise is therefore particularly relevant to parents who want their children to develop consistent examination preparation habits rather than leaving revision until close to the BECE.
Early preparation can give students more time to improve
Mock examinations can be useful when they are treated as part of preparation rather than simply as tests.
A student who performs poorly in a particular subject or struggles with a particular type of question still has time to work on those weaknesses when the mock is taken early enough.
This makes early participation useful for students who may need additional practice. Rather than waiting until the final examination is approaching, parents can identify areas that require attention and encourage their wards to work on them over time.
The objective is not simply to obtain a mock score. The more important benefit is to use the exercise as part of an ongoing preparation process.
The BECE Home Mock also helps parents monitor progress
Parents often want to know whether their wards are making progress with their BECE preparation.
The Home Mock provides an organised avenue through which parents can participate in that process.
After a student takes the mock, parents can review the student’s performance and use the results to guide further revision and practice.
Where weaknesses are identified, parents can encourage additional study in those areas. Where a student performs well, the result can provide an indication of areas in which the student is becoming more comfortable.
This makes the mock useful not only to students but also to parents who want to remain actively involved in their children’s examination preparation.
2026 October BECE Home Mock: Who should consider enrolling?
The Home Mock is intended for BECE preparation, and parents can use the programme to give their wards additional examination practice.
It can be particularly useful for students who are beginning their preparation early, students who want regular practice before the BECE and students whose parents want a structured way of monitoring their preparation.
The emphasis is on continuous preparation rather than waiting until the examination is close.
How to register for the BECE Home Mock
Parents can register their wards through the BECE Home Mock portal:
BECE Home Mock Portal:
https://skulmanager.com/Homemock/index.php
The registration process begins on the Home Mock portal, where parents can select the appropriate package for their ward.
The available options are structured according to the number of subjects a student wants to take.

How parents can access the mock papers
After registering, parents and students can use the Home Mock portal to access the relevant mock examination materials provided for the programme.
Parents should keep the registration details used for the ward because these may be required when accessing the student’s mock activities and materials.
The portal serves as the main point for managing participation in the Home Mock, so parents should return to it for the relevant examination information and access arrangements.
What parents can do on the BECE Home Mock portal
The BECE Home Mock portal is designed to provide parents with a central point for managing their wards’ participation in the programme.
Depending on the stage of the mock exercise, parents can use the portal to:
Register their wards for the BECE Home Mock.
Select an appropriate subject package.
Complete the enrolment process.
Access the relevant mock examination materials.
Follow the instructions provided for participating in the mock.
Download questions, schemes, marked scripts and examiner’s report
Use the available information to support their wards’ examination preparation.
Parents should carefully follow the instructions displayed on the portal during registration and when accessing the mock papers.
Why parents should not wait until the final BECE preparation period
For many students, examination preparation becomes more effective when it is spread over a longer period rather than concentrated immediately before the examination.
An early mock gives students an opportunity to practise, assess their preparedness and identify areas that need more work.
It also gives parents more time to respond to identified weaknesses.
For example, if a student discovers through mock practice that a particular subject or area is difficult, the family can incorporate additional revision into the student’s preparation instead of discovering the weakness much later.
The October Home Mock can therefore form part of a longer preparation cycle for students who are taking their BECE preparation seriously.
A mock examination is more useful when students learn from it
Parents are encouraged to look beyond the score after their wards complete the mock.
The most useful outcome is what the student does with the performance afterwards.
Students should review areas where they made mistakes, continue practising difficult topics and use subsequent preparation opportunities to improve.
Parents can support this process by discussing the results with their wards and helping them maintain a consistent revision routine.
The mock should therefore be viewed as a preparation tool, not simply another examination.
